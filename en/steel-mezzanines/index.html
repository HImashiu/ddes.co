Design of steel mezzanines and intermediate floors

More usable floor area inside the same warehouse or store.

Home/Solutions/Steel mezzanine

A steel mezzanine adds a level inside a warehouse, a logistics center, a store or a plant. Its design defines beams, columns, deck, stairs and railings for the NSR-10 live loads (B.4.2), the racking and the equipment it will carry, and checks that the existing structure and foundation can take it. DDES designs it and follows its erection.

What to have at hand

  • Available area and height, and the location of existing columns.
  • Use of the mezzanine: offices, storage or racking.
  • If it carries racking: height, number of levels and maximum weight per pallet or per level.
  • Equipment that will move on the mezzanine, such as pallet jacks or carts, with its loaded weight.
  • Drawings of the warehouse or store, if they exist.
  • Photos of the floor slab where the columns will sit.
  • How goods will get up there: stairs, a vertical lift, a crane or a pallet truck.
  • If a forklift will drive under the mezzanine, the clear height it needs.

How we solve it

  1. Review of photos and drawings, or a site visit: area, clear height, existing columns and floor slab.
  2. Definition of use and loads: Table B.4.2.1-1 live load, racking, equipment and impact (B.4.4).
  3. Choice of system (freestanding, rack-supported or tied to the building) and of deck type.
  4. Design of steel beams, columns, connections and bracing (Title F), with the seismic hazard of the municipality.
  5. Check of the floor slab and foundation under each column; new footings where needed.
  6. Calculations, structural drawings, shop and erection drawings, and support during erection.

What load must a mezzanine carry?

The NSR-10 sets minimums in Table B.4.2.1-1: 2.0 kN/m² (200 kgf/m²) for offices, 6.0 kN/m² (600 kgf/m²) for light storage and 12.0 kN/m² (1,200 kgf/m²) for heavy storage. They are minimums: the code itself (B.4.2.1) requires designing for the maximum load expected from the use, so if the goods weigh more, that value governs. That is why an office mezzanine cannot become storage without reviewing the design.

What changes in a logistics mezzanine?

Besides the distributed load, concentrated loads matter: rack legs, stacked pallets and equipment moving on the deck. The design locates them, checks the deck and beams under each support and defines where equipment can travel. If the racking holds up the mezzanine, racking and floor are designed as one system.

And for an industrial plant?

Table B.4.2.1-1 gives 5.0 kN/m² (500 kgf/m²) for light industry and 10.0 kN/m² (1,000 kgf/m²) for heavy industry. Machines are added with their actual weight and, if they vibrate, that effect is checked in beams and connections.

Can it bear on the existing floor slab?

Only if the slab and the soil can take each column's concentrated load. If not, new footings are designed under the columns.

What types of mezzanine are there?

There are three ways to hold it up. A freestanding mezzanine has its own columns and does not bear on the building: it is the easiest to move or extend. A rack-supported mezzanine uses the racking itself as structure, so racking and floor are designed together and no bay can be removed without a check. A mezzanine tied to the building bears on existing columns or walls, and then the building structure has to be checked for the new weight and forces.

Which deck works best?

Composite steel deck with concrete gives a stiff slab for heavy loads and wheeled equipment, at the cost of more weight. Steel grating is light and lets light, air and sprinkler water through, but does not always suit small wheels. Board decking on steel sections is light and quick to install, and works for offices and low loads if it meets the live load. The deck is chosen with the use, because it changes the self-weight and the size of beams and columns.

How does earthquake design come in?

A mezzanine is a structure that moves in an earthquake. If it is freestanding, it needs its own system to resist horizontal forces, with frames or bracing designed for the seismic hazard of the municipality. If it is tied to the building, it adds mass and forces, and the work on the existing building falls under chapter A.10 of NSR-10, which governs adding to, modifying or remodelling the structural system of existing buildings.

What do railings and stairs require?

Under NSR-10 (B.4.2.2), railings, stair handrails and parapets must resist a horizontal force of 1.00 kN/m (100 kgf/m) applied at the top and carry it to the structure. Intermediate rails and infill panels must resist 0.25 kN (25 kgf) applied over an area no larger than 0.3 m on a side. That is why the railing is designed with its anchors to the beams rather than bought separately.

What about lifts, carts and machines?

NSR-10 (B.4.4) increases the live load where there is impact: 100% at the supports of elevators and lifts, 20% at the supports of light machinery driven by an electric motor, and no less than 50% for reciprocating or piston-engine machinery. B.4.3 also requires checking partial loading: in a warehouse some areas fill up while others stay empty, and that pattern can be worse than a fully loaded floor.

Can the design load be reduced?

The NSR-10 live load reduction by area (B.4.5.1) only applies when the influence area is 35 m² or more and the live load is between 1.80 and 3.00 kN/m². An office mezzanine can use it; a storage mezzanine, at 6.0 or 12.0 kN/m², cannot.

Mistakes we often see

Buying a standard mezzanine without knowing the load it will carry. Setting columns on the floor slab without checking it. Designing beams and columns but leaving connections and bracing unresolved. Changing the use later, for example from offices to storage. Forgetting the clear height the forklift underneath needs. All of them are avoided when the design starts from the real use.

Frequently asked questions

Does a mezzanine need a permit?

A mezzanine adds built area and modifies the structure, so the curaduría urbana usually requires a permit with a signed structural design. Confirm it with your curaduría.

What does DDES deliver?

Calculations, structural drawings, and shop and erection drawings for the fabricator, signed by a licensed civil engineer. If the fabricator already has a design, DDES can review it.

Do you design mezzanines in Bogotá and other cities?

Yes. DDES works from Barranquilla on projects across Colombia. Each city's seismic hazard goes into the design of columns, bracing and connections.

How long does erection take?

The steel is fabricated in a shop and erected quickly; the time depends on size and on the deck chosen.

Has DDES done mezzanines?

Yes. At Bodegas Centurión the works DDES followed included the warehouses' mezzanine.

Can I put racking on an office mezzanine?

Not without a check. NSR-10 asks for 2.0 kN/m² for offices and 6.0 or 12.0 kN/m² for light or heavy storage, and rack legs concentrate the load. Before changing the use, the deck, beams, columns and supports have to be checked.

Can the fabricator design the mezzanine?

The structural design is signed by a civil engineer authorized under Law 400 of 1997. Many fabricators work with one; if you already have a fabricator's design, DDES can review it before fabrication.

What does the design cost depend on?

On the area, the use and loads, whether the existing structure or slab must be checked, and whether shop drawings are needed. With the area, height and use, DDES sends you a proposal.
